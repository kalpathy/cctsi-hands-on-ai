Module Two  ·  The cookbook

Small, safe automations

Twenty-three chores that come round again and again, each turned into a fixed input, fixed steps, and an output you check. No programming. Most of them run in Microsoft Copilot, which everyone at CU already has.

01

How to read a recipe

Every recipe has the same shape as the biosketch work in this module: what goes in, the fixed steps, what comes out, and what you check. The last part is not optional. A recipe saves you the typing, not the reading.

Small means one job, done the same way every time. Safe means the inputs are yours to share, nothing leaves CU's approved tools, and a person reads the output before it goes anywhere.

Each recipe is tagged with the tool it needs, and who has that tool:

ToolWho has it at CUGood for
Copilot Chat Everyone, free. Sign in with your CU account at copilot.cloud.microsoft, or use it in Outlook and Teams. Web search is on by default. Drafting, summarizing, reworking files you upload
Copilot, paid A paid Microsoft 365 Copilot license, requested through OIT Copilot inside Word, Excel and PowerPoint; prompts that run on a schedule
Power Automate Everyone, free for your own flows with the standard connectors Things that should happen without you: saving, logging, reminding

Google license A paid CU Google Workspace license, which includes Gemini and NotebookLM (now called Gemini Notebook) Notebooks over sources you chose, as in Module 1

Checked against CU's pages on 7 October 2026. These change. The authority is CU's AI tool overview and comparison.

The rules for every recipe

No PHI in any of these. If a chore involves patient data, ask the data owner and ISS before automating it.

Sign in with your CU account, never a personal one, for anything that is not already public.

Read before it leaves your hands. Anything you send, submit or sign is yours, whoever drafted it.

02

Your record, reused

Start from the record builder. Once your record is true, every one of these is a view of it.

01

A 150-word bio

Copilot Chat

For a seminar announcement, an award nomination, a program book.

In
Your CU-format CV from the record builder, copied from the page
Out
A third-person bio of about 150 words
Prompt
Using only the CV below, write a third-person bio of about 150 words for [the event or award].
Include my current title and organization, two research themes, and up to three honors.
Use only facts that appear in the CV. Do not add degrees, titles, numbers or honors that are not there.

CV:
[paste your CV]

You check every title, date and honor appears in your CV, and nothing was upgraded.

02

A Personal Statement for a new project

Copilot Chator ChatGPT Edu

Every new application needs one, and each one is about a different project.

  1. In the record builder, paste the new aims and press Rank my publications.
  2. Press Fill missing DOIs and PMIDs, then Build the grounded prompt.
  3. Paste the prompt into Copilot and add the line below.
Add to the end of the grounded prompt
Write only the Personal Statement for now, at most 3,500 characters including spaces.
Explain why I am suited to my role on THIS project, using the aims above.
After the text, list every PMID you referred to.

You check every PMID is one of your ten, and count the characters yourself in Word (Review, Word Count, characters with spaces).

03

Trim to the limit

Copilot Chat

The draft is good and 400 characters too long.

Prompt
Shorten this Contribution to Science to under 2,000 characters including spaces.
Keep every (PMID: n) reference exactly as it is. Do not add any claim, paper or number.
If something must go, cut description before you cut a result.
Then list what you removed.

[paste the Contribution]

You check the count yourself, and that every PMID survived unchanged. Models are poor at counting characters.

04

Honors, newest first

Copilot Chat

The Supplement allows up to 15. Your CV has more, in no particular order.

Prompt
From the CV below, list my honors and awards, newest first, one per line, as:
YEAR  Award, awarding body
Use only items in the CV. If there are more than 15, list the 15 most recent and then name the rest separately.

CV:
[paste your CV]

You check each line against the CV, and choose the 15 yourself. Most recent is a default, not a judgment.

05

Publications since a date

Copilot Chator Copilot in Excel

For a progress report, an annual review, a department newsletter.

In
career-record.json, downloaded from the record builder
Out
A table of publications from a given year onward
Prompt, with the file attached
The attached file is my career record. From its "publications" list, make a table of every
publication with year [2024] or later: title, journal, year, PMID, DOI. Newest first.
Use only entries in the file. Then tell me how many rows the table has.

You check the count against ORCID for the same years. If they differ, find the missing row before you use the table.

06

The PI pre-flight check

Record builder + Copilot in Outlook

For coordinators: find what a PI's record is missing weeks before the deadline, not the day of it.

  1. Run the PI's ORCID iD in the record builder. Copy the findings under What is missing.
  2. In Outlook, open a new email to the PI and ask Copilot to draft it from the prompt below.
  3. Press Start a new record before the next PI.
Prompt
Draft a short, friendly email to [PI name] before their [grant] submission due [date].
Explain that NIH builds part of the biosketch from their ORCID record, and list these
items for them to fix in ORCID, most important first. Offer to help. Do not add any item.

Items:
[paste the findings]

You check the list matches the findings exactly. You can find the problems; only the PI can certify the biosketch.

Email and meetings

07

A long thread, in five lines

Copilot in Outlook

Forty replies, three decisions, and one thing someone is waiting on you for.

Prompt, with the thread open
Summarize this thread as three lists: decisions made; open questions; and who owes what, by when.
For each item, say which email it comes from (sender and date).
If something is unclear in the thread, say so rather than resolving it.

You check any item you will act on by opening the email it names.

08

The biosketch chase email

Copilot in Outlook

For coordinators: the email to every key person on an application, sent once and sent early.

Prompt
Draft an email to the key personnel on [grant, sponsor, due date]. Ask each person to send
me a certified NIH biosketch from SciENcv by [your internal date]. Include this checklist:
- made in SciENcv, not a Word template, and not re-saved or flattened afterwards
- ORCID iD linked to their eRA Commons account
- research security training completed in the last 12 months
- Personal Statement written for THIS project
Keep it under 150 words. Friendly, clear, no exclamation marks.

You check the dates, the grant, and that the checklist matches the current NIH instructions for this application.

09

Notes from a meeting transcript

Copilot Chat

The lab meeting was recorded and transcribed. Nobody took notes.

In
The transcript file, downloaded from the Teams meeting
Out
Decisions, action items with owners, and what was left open
Prompt, with the transcript attached
From the attached transcript, list: decisions made; action items with the owner and any date
mentioned; and questions left unresolved. Use only the transcript. Where the transcript is
ambiguous about who agreed to what, write [UNCLEAR] instead of guessing.

You check each action item against the transcript, and send owners their items to confirm. Only for meetings where everyone knew it was recorded, and never for meetings that discuss patients.

10

A Monday digest

Copilot, paid

One prompt, scheduled to run every Monday morning, so the week starts with a list.

  1. Run the prompt once in Copilot and adjust it until the answer is useful.
  2. Use the option to schedule the prompt (paid license, up to 10 schedules).
Prompt
From my email in the last 7 days, list messages about [study name], [IRB], or [grant number]
that still need a reply or an action from me. One line each: sender, date, what they need.
Oldest first.

You check the messages themselves before acting. A digest can miss an email; it is a reminder, not a record.

Documents

11

What changed between two versions

Word Compare+ Copilot Chat

A protocol, aims page or manuscript came back edited, without track changes.

  1. In Word: Review, Compare, Compare two versions. This step is not AI, and it is exact.
  2. Save the compared document, attach it in Copilot, and use the prompt.
Prompt, with the compared file attached
The attached document shows tracked changes between two versions. List the changes of
substance (meaning, numbers, dates, eligibility, procedures) in the order they appear,
quoting the changed text. Ignore formatting and punctuation. Do not judge whether they are good.

You check the summary against the tracked changes, which are the ground truth. Use the exact tool for the part that has to be exact.

12

A lay summary

Copilot Chat

For a community advisory board, a newsletter, a recruitment flyer draft.

Prompt
Rewrite this abstract for a general audience, about 120 words, at roughly an 8th-grade reading
level. Do not add any finding, number or claim that is not in the abstract, and do not make
the results sound more certain than the abstract does.

[paste the abstract]

You check every number and claim maps back to the abstract. Anything for participants also goes through the IRB.

13

Your record as a Word CV

Copilot Chator Copilot in Word

The record builder renders your CV on the page. Promotion committees want a Word file.

Prompt
Turn the CV below into a Word document. Keep every section heading and every entry, in the
same order, with years on the left. Do not rewrite, merge or drop any entry.
Then tell me how many entries each section has.

[paste the CV from the record builder]

You check the per-section counts against the page. A dropped line is the failure you will not notice by reading.

05

Spreadsheets and trackers

14

Grants ending this year

Copilot Chator Copilot in Excel

Which awards need a renewal, a no-cost extension, or a final report soon.

In
Your RePORTER export (the same CSV the record builder takes)
Out
A short table of projects ending in the next 12 months
Prompt, with the CSV attached
From the attached RePORTER export, list projects whose project end date falls within 12 months
of [today's date]: project number, title, end date. Sort by end date.
Tell me which column you used for the end date and how many projects matched.

You check by sorting the end-date column yourself. The count must match.

15

A funding deadline tracker

Copilot Chat

Five funding notices, five sets of dates and limits, one table.

Prompt, with the notices attached or pasted
From the funding notices below, make a table: notice number, title, application due date(s),
eligibility in one line, page limits, budget limit. For every date and limit, quote the
sentence it comes from. If a field is not stated, write NOT STATED.

[paste or attach the notices]

You check every date against the notice itself. Dates are where models slip, and a wrong due date costs a cycle.

16

Clean a messy list

Copilot in Excelor Copilot Chat

A roster, a sign-up export, a list of collaborators: names in three formats and duplicates.

Prompt
Clean this table: put names as Last, First; split any combined name-and-email column;
standardize dates as YYYY-MM-DD. Do not delete any row. Add a column called CHANGED that
says what you changed in each row, and a column called POSSIBLE_DUPLICATE.

You check the row count before and after is the same, and review every POSSIBLE_DUPLICATE yourself.

06

Automations that run themselves

Power Automate (make.powerautomate.com, CU sign-in) runs small flows of the form when this happens, do that. No AI is involved once a flow is built, which is exactly why it is reliable. Start from a template, or, if your account offers it, describe the flow in a sentence and let Copilot build the first draft.

Use flows for your own, non-sensitive files and messages. Ask ISS before automating anything that touches patient or restricted data.

17

Save attachments automatically

Power Automate

Reports from a core facility or a sponsor arrive by email and get lost there.

Describe the flow
When a new email arrives in my Outlook inbox from [sender address] with an attachment,
save each attachment to the OneDrive folder [Reports/Core facility], with the date in the file name.

You check by sending one test email, then looking in the folder after the first week.

18

A form that logs itself

Power AutomateMicrosoft Forms

Seminar sign-ups, data requests, equipment bookings: responses straight into a spreadsheet, with a heads-up.

Describe the flow
When a new response is submitted to my Microsoft Form [form name], add a row to the table
[Requests] in the Excel file [file] on OneDrive with every answer and the time, then send me
an email with the requester's name and their first answer.

You check by submitting a test response. Do not collect anything in the form you would not want in an email.

19

The biosketch collection box

Power Automate

For coordinators: one folder where key personnel drop their biosketches, and you hear about each one.

Describe the flow
When a file is added to the SharePoint or OneDrive folder [Biosketches - grant name],
post a message to the Teams channel [channel] saying the file name and who added it,
and add a row to the Excel table [Received] with the file name, person and date.

You check folder permissions first (only the people who should add files can), then add a test file.

20

A reminder that sends itself

Power Automate

The weekly lab checklist, the monthly effort reminder, the quarterly report nudge.

Describe the flow
Every Monday at 8:00 am Mountain time, post this message in the Teams channel [lab channel]:
[your checklist]

You check the first run, and put an end date in your calendar to review whether it is still needed.

Set it up once, reuse it

21

A ChatGPT Edu Project for your biosketch

ChatGPT Edu

The same files and the same rules, every application, without pasting them again.

  1. Create a Project. Add career-record.json, your CV, and current aims as files.
  2. Paste the RULES section of the grounded prompt into the Project's instructions.
  3. For each new application, start a chat in the Project and add the new aims.

Use Projects, not custom GPTs: OpenAI has announced it is retiring custom GPTs for Edu workspaces, planned for December 2026.

You check every draft the same way as today. Keep the files current: a Project holding last year's CV drafts last year's biosketch.

22

A Copilot agent that checks drafts

Copilot Chat

A second pair of eyes with the rules built in, for you or for the PIs you support.

  1. In Copilot Chat, create an agent and paste these instructions.
  2. To use it, paste your ten Products and the draft.
Agent instructions
You check NIH Biosketch Supplement drafts. The user gives you a list of up to ten Products and
a draft. Do not rewrite anything. Report:
1. Every reference in the draft, in order, and whether it matches an item in the list.
2. Anything that looks like a full citation, a reference list, or a link (NIH allows none).
3. The character count of each section against its limit: Personal Statement 3,500;
   each Contribution to Science 2,000.
4. Every claim about the author's role (led, designed, first) so the author can confirm it.

You check anyway. A model checking a model's work is a filter, not the check.

23

A policy desk notebook

Google license

Module 1's notebook, pointed at the NIH rules you keep looking up.

  1. Create a notebook in NotebookLM (Gemini Notebook).
  2. Add as sources: NOT-OD-26-018, NOT-OD-26-079, the Biographical Sketch Common Form page, and the NIH Common Forms FAQ, as URLs.
  3. Ask your question the way you would ask a grants administrator.

You check by clicking the citation, as in Module 1. When NIH issues a new notice, add it, or the notebook answers from old rules.

Make your own

Pick a chore you did three times last month. Write down what goes in, what comes out, and how you would know it was wrong. If you cannot answer the last one, it is not ready to automate.
