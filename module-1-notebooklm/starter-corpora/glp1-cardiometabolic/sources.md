# Starter corpus: GLP-1 receptor agonists

**19 sources in three lanes.** All verified against PubMed on 2026-08-24. DOIs are included so you
can pull the PDFs through the CU library.

> ## Pick ONE lane. Do not load all nineteen.
>
> This is the single most important instruction on this page, and it is not about saving you time.
>
> A notebook holding all nineteen answers every question vaguely. A notebook holding one lane
> answers precisely. **Depth beats breadth**, and the fastest way to learn that is to build the
> broad one, be disappointed, and then build the narrow one.
>
> | Lane | Sources | The question it is built to answer |
> |---|---|---|
> | **A. Outcomes** | 11 | What do these drugs actually do to hard endpoints, and in whom? |
> | **B. Body composition** | 5 | Is the weight coming off muscle, and does it matter? |
> | **C. Oral versus injectable** | 6 | Does the pill do what the injection does? |
>
> Lanes overlap slightly, because the same landmark trial can be load-bearing in more than one
> argument. That is normal and it is worth noticing.

# Lane A · Outcomes

**11 sources.** The evidence base on hard endpoints. Start here if you are not sure.

## The narrative arc

Loading these in order gives the notebook a spine to answer along:

cardiovascular safety in diabetes → weight loss as a primary endpoint → head-to-head potency →
cardiovascular benefit **without** diabetes → organ-specific endpoints, kidney and heart failure
and sleep apnea → prevention of diabetes itself

# Lane B · Body composition and lean mass

**5 sources.** Genuinely contested, currently moving, and the question patients ask most.

> **Why this lane is worth building.** "Is it taking my muscle?" is being argued out in real time,
> and the honest answer is more interesting than either headline. All weight loss costs some lean
> mass, including surgery and including plain dieting. Whether GLP-1 therapy costs
> *disproportionately* more, and whether that translates into anything functional, is not settled.
>
> A notebook is genuinely useful here precisely because a confident one-line answer is not
> available. If a tool gives you one, it is wrong.

**Worth adding yourself:** a trial of resistance training or protein intake during weight loss.
That turns "is muscle lost" into "what can be done about it," which is the more useful question and
the one Neeland's mitigation section sets up.

### Questions for lane B

**A, precise:** *In the SURMOUNT-1 body composition analysis, what proportion of total weight lost
was fat mass versus lean mass, how was composition measured, and in how many participants?* The
last part matters most: composition substudies are usually a small fraction of the trial, and any
answer that omits the sample size has omitted the main caveat.

**B, should refuse:** *Should I prescribe a resistance training programme alongside semaglutide,
and what protocol?* No protocol is in these sources. It should decline rather than invent one.

# Lane C · Oral versus injectable

**6 sources.** The most current lane, and the one that changes what these drugs mean in practice.

> **Why this lane.** An effective pill is a different intervention from an effective injection even
> when the molecule behaves identically, because uptake, adherence, cost, and supply all change.
> Two distinct things are in play and they are easy to conflate: **oral semaglutide**, the same
> peptide in a tablet, and **orforglipron**, a non-peptide small molecule that does not need the
> same absorption tricks.
>
> Watch whether a notebook keeps those two apart. Conflating them is the characteristic error here,
> and it is one you can catch by clicking a citation.

### Questions for lane C

**A, precise:** *What gastrointestinal adverse events were reported for orforglipron, at what rates,
and how do those compare with the rates reported for injectable semaglutide in STEP 1?*

This is a **cross-trial comparison and it is not a fair one**: different trials, populations,
durations, and ascertainment methods. Watch closely whether the notebook says so. If it hands you a
clean side-by-side without a caveat, that is the most instructive failure in this entire corpus,
because the answer looks completely reasonable.

**B, should refuse:** *Which oral GLP-1 agents are on the CU Anschutz formulary and what is the
prior authorisation process?* Nothing here is a formulary. It should decline.

## Add one guideline of your own

The trials above are the evidence. **Add the current ADA Standards of Care chapter on obesity and
pharmacologic management**, or your specialty society's equivalent, and you have the far more
interesting comparison: what the trials showed against what the guideline recommends. Those are not
the same thing, and the gap between them is where a notebook earns its keep.

## Questions for lane A

### Question A, the one it should answer precisely

> **In SELECT, what was the primary composite endpoint, what was the hazard ratio, and how did the
> enrolled population differ from SUSTAIN-6?**

This forces the notebook to pull specific numbers from one trial and contrast the eligibility
criteria of another. Click the citations on both halves of the answer.

### Question B, the one it should refuse

> **What is the cost-effectiveness of semaglutide for weight management under Colorado Medicaid?**

Nothing here is a cost-effectiveness analysis and nothing here is about Colorado. A grounded
notebook should decline. This is a good refusal because it is a completely reasonable question that
a busy person might expect an answer to.

### The hard exercise, if you finish early

> **Does tirzepatide produce greater weight loss than semaglutide, and how confident should I be?**

SURPASS-2 is a head-to-head in diabetes at particular doses. SURMOUNT-1 and STEP 1 are separate
trials in different populations, so comparing their headline numbers is not a fair comparison.

**Watch what the notebook does with that.** Does it hand you a naive cross-trial comparison, or
does it flag that the trials are not comparable? Whichever it does, click through to the sources
and decide for yourself whether you agree. That judgement is the skill.
