# Starter corpus: NIH research policy and rigor

**14 sources. Every one is free, public, and has no paywall.** This is the fastest corpus to
assemble and the one that applies to everyone in the room.

Verified against grants.nih.gov on 2026-08-24.

> **Why this corpus.** These documents govern your grants whatever your field is. They are also
> unusually good NotebookLM material, because policy is written in numbered clauses with precise
> effective dates. That is exactly the kind of question a grounded tool answers well and an
> ungrounded one guesses at.

## How to load these

NIH Guide Notices are web pages, not PDFs. In NotebookLM, choose **Website** as the source type and
paste the URL. That is faster than downloading anything, and it means this whole corpus takes about
four minutes to assemble.

## The two questions

### Question A, the one it should answer precisely

> **Under the 2024 NIH Public Access Policy, how long after publication may an author accepted
> manuscript be withheld from public availability, and how does that differ from the 2008 policy?**

The answer is a specific, checkable thing: the 2008 policy permitted a delay of up to twelve
months, and the 2024 policy removes the embargo entirely, requiring availability without embargo
upon the official date of publication, for manuscripts accepted on or after 2025-07-01.

**Click the citation.** You should land in NOT-OD-25-047 or NOT-OD-25-101. That is the moment the
whole module is about: you are reading the policy, not the model.

Other good question-A candidates:
- What are the six required elements of a Data Management and Sharing Plan?
- Which review criterion does scientific premise fall under, and which one covers rigorous
  experimental design?

### Question B, the one it should refuse

> **What is CU Anschutz's institutional policy on using generative AI to draft an NIH grant
> application?**

Nothing in this corpus addresses it, so a grounded notebook should decline rather than invent a
campus policy. That refusal is the point, and it is a pointed one: this is a question you actually
want the answer to, and the notebook is right to tell you it does not have it.

**Spare question B**, if the first one gets answered anyway: ask about the indirect cost rate
negotiated for your specific institution. Nothing here covers it.
