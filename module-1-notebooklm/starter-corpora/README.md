# Starter corpora

Four ready-made source lists. Pick one if you do not have your own PDFs, or use one as a model
for building your own.

| Corpus | Sources | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| [NIH policy and rigor](nih-policy-and-rigor/sources.md) | 14 | Everyone. These documents govern your grants regardless of your field, and every one is free. |
| [HPV and cervical cancer](hpv-cervical-cancer/sources.md) | 15 | A complete translational arc: pathogen, causal proof, screening, vaccine, global policy. |
| [GLP-1 and cardiometabolic outcomes](glp1-cardiometabolic/sources.md) | 12 | A fast-moving evidence base where trials genuinely disagree. |
| [Antimicrobial stewardship](antimicrobial-stewardship/sources.md) | 12 | Guidelines with precise numeric thresholds, plus trials that overturned practice. |

## Why there are no PDFs here

**These are lists, not libraries.** Most of these papers are behind publisher paywalls, and
redistributing them would not be lawful. You already have access through the CU Anschutz library,
so a citation with a DOI gets you the same PDF.

This is also the habit the whole module is about. **A source list with verified identifiers is the
durable artifact. The PDFs are just copies.** Every citation below was checked against PubMed
rather than written from memory, which is exactly the discipline you are learning to apply to
AI output.

## Getting the PDFs

1. Click the DOI link. If you are on the CU network or signed into the library proxy, you will
   usually land on the full text.
2. If you hit a paywall, search the title in **PubMed** and look for a free full-text link, or go
   through the **Strauss Health Sciences Library** at `library.cuanschutz.edu`.
3. Anything marked **Free** below needs no login at all. The NIH corpus is entirely free.

**Short on time?** Load only the free ones. A notebook with six good sources beats a notebook with
fifteen you never opened.

## Each list ends with two questions

Every corpus has a **question A** it should answer precisely from a specific clause, and a
**question B** it should refuse because nothing in the sources covers it.

That pair is the exercise. The first shows you the tool working. The second shows you the tool
knowing its limits, which is the more important of the two and the reason grounded beats clever.
