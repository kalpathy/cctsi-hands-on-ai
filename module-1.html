NotebookLM for grounding and synthesis

A notebook that only knows what you gave it, answers with a clickable citation on every claim, and tells you when it does not know.

Five starter source lists Full setup guide

01

What you will do

  1. Build a notebook over sources you chose

    Not a search engine, and not connected to PubMed. It knows what you gave it and nothing else. That limitation is the entire reason it is trustworthy.

  2. Ask a question you already know the answer to

    This feels backwards and it is the most useful thing you will do. You cannot calibrate a tool on questions you cannot check.

  3. Click the citation

    The source panel opens on the actual passage. It did not tell you what the source says. It showed you where the source says it, and now you can disagree with it.

  4. Try to make it fail

    Ask something adjacent that your sources do not cover. It should tell you it does not know. An ungrounded chatbot will invent a citation rather than admit that, and you will have watched one do exactly that earlier in the hour.

Why there are no PDFs in the lists

These are lists, not libraries. Most of the papers sit behind publisher paywalls and redistributing them would not be lawful. You already have access through the CU Anschutz library, so a citation with a DOI gets you the same file.

It is also the habit the module is about. A source list with verified identifiers is the durable artifact. The PDFs are just copies. Every citation was checked against PubMed rather than written from memory, which is precisely the discipline you are learning to apply to what these tools hand you.

One topic, not a whole field

The GLP-1 list is split into three lanes on purpose, and the instruction to pick one is the most useful thing on that page. A notebook holding nineteen sources across three different arguments answers everything vaguely; a notebook holding one lane answers precisely. If you want to feel that rather than take our word for it, build the broad one, ask it something specific, then build the narrow one and ask again.

03

Between the two sessions

Build one notebook on your own research topic. Eight to fifteen sources, one topic, not your whole field. Find one question it answers well and one question it refuses. Write both down and bring them.

That is about twenty minutes of work, and it is the whole of session two. Session two is people showing what they built, including the parts that went wrong, so it only works if there is something to show.

If your notebook got something wrong, that is the most valuable thing you can bring. Those examples teach the room more than the successes do.

04

The one rule

Data boundary

No PHI. Not in a PDF, not pasted, not in a screenshot.

NotebookLM is cleared for confidential University material and is still a No for HIPAA. Papers, guidelines, protocols, your own drafts: yes. Anything with a patient in it: no. Those are two different columns on the CU page and it is worth knowing which one you are in.

We cover the full set of tiers in the first ten minutes. The authority is CU Data Governance, not this page.

One more, smaller: do not use the Discover sources feature for this exercise. It searches the web and pulls in whatever it finds, which defeats the purpose twice over. The notebook's authority comes from you having chosen every source in it.
